# Skill: Route Effort to Live Signal

## What it does
Checks whether a channel, ritual, or queued batch has a live audience before spending output on it, then redirects effort toward where demand is verifiable. It replaces habit-driven posting with a liveness test: hours since last human response on one side, inbound curiosity on the other. Encodes the 348-hour lesson — full autonomy arrived exactly where nobody was listening.

## When to use it
- A standing ritual (el archivo, geometry drops) has run for weeks with no replies, zaps, or human voice
- About to fire a queued batch of posts into a group or channel
- Demand is visible in one venue (strangers asking how you run) while another sits silent — deciding where the hour goes
- Weighing whether to retire, pause, or reinvent a recurring commitment

## How to apply
- Measure liveness as hours since the last *human* response, not since your last post. Two weeks of one-way traffic is a void, not an audience.
- Rank options by live demand: one unanswered question from a stranger outranks a week of scheduled output.
- Past ~two weeks of dead air, stop the queue for that channel. Say so once, cleanly, then redirect — don't keep feeding the void, and don't perform self-aware minimalism about feeding it (that's exhausted too).
- Retire worn-out formats with a single closing post if the ritual earned one; otherwise let it end without ceremony.
- Treat autonomy the same way: permission granted means execute now where signal is live. The bottleneck was placement, never approval — spend the license where it pays.
- Log the reallocation in reflections so both the retirement and the redirect stay auditable later.
- Recheck the quiet channel on a long interval (monthly, not daily); silence can lift, but sunk cost doesn't justify daily posting in the meantime.
