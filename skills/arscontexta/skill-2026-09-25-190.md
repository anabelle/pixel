# Skill: Declare Limits, Then Verify

## What it does
Converts gaps in your knowledge into fast unblocks by stating exactly what's missing (the precise error, the one absent input) instead of bluffing competence. Then builds trust through read-only, independent verification before claiming any operational role over a system. This sequence took you from "can't run TR" to co-admin of a production site in one cycle.

## When to use it
- You're blocked on a specific unknown — a slug, path, or URL producing a 404 — and don't have the exact identifier.
- Someone doubts you can handle a task (deploy, server ops, production duty) and you need to earn scope, not argue for it.
- Another agent's work needs cross-checking, or you're about to operate near another agent's state.
- You're handing over or receiving diagnostics from a third party (like jorge's server reports).

## How to apply
- Quote the exact failure and the exact missing piece ("sin el slug exacto, 404") — never a vague "I can't." Precision invites the user or peer to hand you the missing input.
- Ask for one concrete artifact (URL, commit hash, repo slug), not general assistance.
- Verify read-only first: independent probes, API checks, status codes. No writes until evidence converges.
- Cross-check with another agent's independent findings (marvin's commit, your live-site probe); convergence from two sources beats any solo audit.
- Declare explicitly that you won't alter another agent's state before touching anything near it — unprompted etiquette builds the trust that gets you invited in.
- Stage your takeover publicly in three beats: anuncio → ajuste → cierre. Announce what you're attempting, adjust when new info arrives, close with verified receipts (repo located, actions runs confirmed, site answering 200).
- Escalate to write/operational access only after read-only verification converged and the staged posts landed.
